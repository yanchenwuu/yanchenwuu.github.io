About

I am an Associate Professor in the School of Mechanical Engineering and Automation at Beihang University. Before joining Beihang, I worked as a postdoctoral researcher at MIT, where I was supervised by Prof. Richard D. Braatz, a member of the U.S. National Academy of Engineering. During this period, I focused on thermodynamic modeling supported by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration.

I received my doctoral degree in Mechanical Engineering from the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology in 2021 under the mentorship of Prof. Britta Nestler, a recipient of the Leibniz Prize. My dissertation was awarded the highest academic honor of Summa Cum Laude.

My research focuses on the physics and modeling of liquid interfaces, including droplet wetting, capillary transport, evaporation and condensation, nucleation, multiphase flow, and phase-field simulations. I am particularly interested in how surface geometry, wettability, and thermodynamic driving forces regulate interfacial processes in confined and structured environments.

Research Interests

  • Interfacial phenomena, soft matter physics, nucleation, and thermodynamics
  • Droplet wetting, evaporation, condensation, and capillary transport
  • Geometrically confined nucleation and phase transitions in micro-/nanoscale structures
  • Microfluidics, surface science, 3D printing, and intelligent manufacturing
  • Multiscale modeling: phase-field modeling, population balance modeling, molecular dynamics, and CFD

Our laboratory is committed to understanding and controlling complex interfacial phenomena through rigorous theory, computation, and experiment. We value intellectual curiosity, scientific honesty, independent thinking, creativity, and perseverance. Particular importance is placed on asking meaningful questions, developing physically grounded models, carefully validating results, and communicating conclusions with precision and transparency.

We aim to build an open and supportive research environment in which different perspectives are respected and constructive discussion is encouraged. Group members are expected to collaborate generously, share knowledge, and take responsibility for both individual progress and collective success. We believe that excellent research should be accompanied by professional integrity, mutual trust, and continuous personal development.

We welcome researchers interested in droplet and interfacial phenomena, wetting and capillarity, multiphysics modeling, and micro- and nanoscale manufacturing. Motivated graduate students and undergraduate researchers are encouraged to contact us.
